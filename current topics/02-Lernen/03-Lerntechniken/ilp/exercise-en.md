# Individual Learning Phase: Structuring and Long-Term Retention of Learning Material from Core Tech Fundamentals

## Your Goal

You will explore a specific topic from Core Tech Fundamentals using several learning techniques, structure the content understandably, and prepare it in such a way that you can sustainably review it.

## What You Need

- Your documents on a current Core Tech Fundamentals topic
- A specialist text, script, slides, or notes of approx. 2–5 pages
- Paper or a digital note-taking tool
- Pens or a mind map tool
- Index cards or a flashcard app
- A timer

## Time Management

- 10 minutes: Task 1
- 25 minutes: Task 2
- 20 minutes: Task 3
- 20 minutes: Task 4
- 25 minutes: Task 5
- 20 minutes: Task 6

## Basic Tasks

### Task 1:

Select learning material and formulate learning questions 

**Goal:** You logically narrow down your learning material and define what you should be able to do confidently at the end.

**Assignment:** Choose a specific topic from your current material, for example, network fundamentals, IP address and subnet mask, DNS, DHCP, CPU and RAM, file systems, or the OSI model. Limit the topic so that you can work on it within 2 hours. Then note down:
- the exact topic section
- the source used
- 5 specific learning questions that you want to be able to answer after processing the material

Formulate the learning questions so that they are technical and verifiable.

**Expected Result / Desired State:** You have a clearly defined topic, a suitable source, and 5 understandable learning questions.

### Task 2:

Apply SQ3R to your learning text 

**Goal:** You explore the text in a structured and active way instead of just reading passively.

**Assignment:** Process your material using the SQ3R method:
- **Survey:** Skim headlines, graphics, tables, terms, and summaries.
- **Question:** Formulate 1–2 questions for each section.
- **Read:** Read the text section by section and only highlight the most important statements.
- **Recite:** Pause briefly after each section and write down what you have understood in your own words.
- **Review:** At the end, check if you can answer your questions.

Work in a clear structure, for example, as a table with the columns: Section, Questions, Key Statements, Own Summary.

**Expected Result / Desired State:** You have a structured SQ3R overview for your learning text with questions, key statements, and your own formulations.

### Task 3:

Break down information with Chunking 

**Goal:** You divide the material into small, learnable units.

**Assignment:** Take your results from Task 2 and break down the material into 5–9 chunks. Each chunk should:
- have a short title
- contain only one clear sub-aspect
- comprise a maximum of 3 key statements
- ideally be noted in your own words

Arrange the chunks logically, for example, from basics to structure, from cause to effect, or from term to application.

**Expected Result / Desired State:** You have a clear list or a grid with 5–9 clearly named chunks that cover the entire topic section.

### Task 4:

Create a mind map from your chunks 

**Goal:** You bring your topic into a visual structure and recognize connections faster.

**Assignment:** Create a mind map based on your chunks. Place your main topic in the center and build the most important branches around it. Add:
- sub-points to each chunk
- central technical terms
- connections between related areas
- markings for particularly important content

Ensure that the mind map doesn't just collect terms but makes the structure of the topic visible.

**Expected Result / Desired State:** You have a readable mind map that hierarchically organizes your topic and shows connections between the contents.

### Task 5:

Create Spaced Repetition material 

**Goal:** You prepare the material in such a way that you can efficiently review it in the coming days and weeks.

**Assignment:** Create 8–12 flashcards from your chunks and your mind map. Use exactly one learning point per card. Use different formats, for example:
- Term → Explanation
- Question → Answer
- Connection → short justification
- Example → suitable technical term

Mark each card according to your current feeling of certainty:
- confident
- partially confident
- unsure

Then set up a simple review plan:
- test briefly again today
- after 1 day
- after 3 days
- after 7 days

**Expected Result / Desired State:** You have 8–12 usable flashcards and a clear review plan for Spaced Repetition.

### Task 6:

Actively retrieve, organize, and connect knowledge 

**Goal:** You check what has really stuck and connect new knowledge with existing prior knowledge.

**Assignment:** Put all documents aside and work only from memory:
1. Write down in 5–7 sentences what your topic is about.
2. Note the 5 most important technical terms, each with a brief explanation.
3. Draw a mini-structure or list from memory.
4. Connect your topic with at least 3 other Core Tech Fundamentals contents that you already know.

Then compare your recall with your documents and mark gaps or uncertain areas.

**Expected Result / Desired State:** You have a brief knowledge summary from memory, recognize your gaps, and have established at least 3 technical connections to other prior knowledge.

## Extension Tasks

### Extension Task 1: Second Mini-Topic in Fast Track

**Goal:** You transfer the methods to another small topic.

**Assignment:** Choose a second, significantly smaller topic section from your material. Process it in a condensed form:
- formulate 3 learning questions
- create 3–5 chunks
- create a small mind map
- formulate 4 flashcards

**Expected Result / Desired State:** You have applied the methods to a second topic and can compare how well the structuring works.

### Extension Task 2: Improve Flashcards

**Goal:** You make your review materials more precise and practical for everyday use.

**Assignment:** Revise your flashcards to make them even more precise. Check:
- Does each card contain only one learning point?
- Is the question unambiguous?
- Is the answer short and technically correct?
- Are there cards you should split?

Then sort the cards into the categories easy, medium, and difficult.

**Expected Result / Desired State:** You have revised, clearly formulated flashcards with a meaningful order of difficulty.

### Extension Task 3: Create a 7-Day Study Plan

**Goal:** You plan the sustainable retention of your knowledge.

**Assignment:** Create a concrete 7-day plan for your topic. Plan short review blocks and define:
- when you review which cards
- when you look at the mind map again
- when you do a recall without documents
- when you work on open gaps

Keep the plan short and realistic so that you can actually implement it.

**Expected Result / Desired State:** You have an actionable 7-day plan for knowledge retention.

## Important Notes

- Work continuously with only one clearly defined main topic.
- Write as much as possible in your own words and avoid mere copying.
- Keep your chunks small and unambiguous.
- Use technical terms consciously, but explain them in a way that you understand them yourself.
- If you notice gaps, mark them visibly instead of skipping them.
- Pay attention to the time and consciously move to the next task after each section.

## Reflection Questions

- Which learning technique helped you the most today and why?
- At which step did you understand your topic best?
- Where did you notice that you only recognized content but didn't truly know it yet?
- Which chunks were easy to learn and which were still too large?
- How useful is your mind map for a later review?
- Which 3 flashcards are currently most important to you?
- When will you actually carry out your first and second review?
